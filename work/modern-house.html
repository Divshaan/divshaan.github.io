Overview

A modern three-storey family home on a 50′ × 120′ lot, taken from a one-paragraph brief to a 12-sheet concept drawing set, a fully furnished 3D model and a two-minute ray-traced walkthrough with an original score.

I wrote the brief and art-directed every stage. The drafting, modelling, rendering and score were produced with Claude (AI), working in CADCraft and Blender.

02

The Challenge

Make a house that reads as buildable, not just pretty — then walk a viewer through all four levels in two minutes without it feeling like a real-estate slideshow. The brief carried the hard parts: about 4,500 sf with a flat roof, double garage, rooftop terrace, studio and finished basement, squeezed onto a narrow suburban lot with real setbacks, floor levels and stair geometry.

Process

From brief to film

03 steps

01

The Brief

A modern, flat-roofed home on a 50′ × 120′ lot: double garage, rooftop terrace, a home studio and a finished basement. Buildable-looking and aware of the building code — a concept, not a permit set.

02

The Drawings

A 12-sheet concept set drafted in CADCraft: site plan, four floor plans, elevations, sections and schedules. 5,838 sf over four levels, a 6.1 m double-height great room and a stacked U-stair. The 3D model is generated from the same spec, so the drawings and the film can’t disagree.

03

The Film

Built and furnished in Blender, then ray-traced in Cycles — 3,420 frames over nine hours. Cut to 28 shots with room labels and an ambient score written in code, whose sections follow the rooms and peak on the roof terrace.

03

Role & credits

I wrote the brief, pushed the film from a real-time preview to full ray-traced lighting, and reviewed every round until sign-off.

Drafting, 3D build, rendering and score: Claude (Anthropic), directed by me. Tools: CADCraft 0.12, Blender 5.2 (Cycles, OptiX), EffectCraft. Furniture, textures and sky: Poly Haven and Quaternius (CC0).

04

What I’d fix next

Building it in 3D exposed what the drawings hid:

  • Bedroom 2’s only window faces the neighbour 1.32 m away — it needs a spatial-separation check.
  • At 5.8 m inside, the garage is tight for two SUVs; 6.1 m would make the house 0.3 m wider.
  • Four levels and no room reserved for an elevator.
  • The cantilever over the entry, the roof spa and the 6 m glass wall all need a structural engineer.
